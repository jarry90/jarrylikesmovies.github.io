Captain America: Brave New World was a pretty enjoyable watch, even though a few things didn’t quite land for me. Anthony Mackie really steps into the role this time. He brings a lot of heart and presence to Sam Wilson, and for the first time it truly feels like he is Captain America. It’s easily his best performance in the MCU so far.

The rest of the cast was solid too. Harrison Ford as President Thaddeus Ross was fine, but he did come across a bit stiff and honestly maybe a little too old for the role. He brings that serious energy you expect from him, but at times it felt like the character needed more physical weight or intensity than Ford could give.

One thing that really pulled me out of the story was the final fight. Sam Wilson, a regular guy without any serum, facing off against a Hulk-level threat just didn’t feel believable. Even for a Marvel film, it was pushing things too far and made the ending harder to take seriously.

I also wasn’t too interested in the part of the story where Sam wrestles with whether or not to take the super soldier serum. It’s a topic we’ve seen before in the MCU and it didn’t bring anything new this time around. Instead of adding depth, it slowed things down.

Another odd choice was the heavy focus on callbacks to the 2008 Incredible Hulk film. The tone and cast are so different that the references just felt out of place. It was a strange decision to rely so much on a movie that a lot of people barely remember or connect with the current MCU.

That said, there is still a lot to like. The action scenes were well done, the movie looked great, and the pacing mostly kept things moving. Red Hulk was a cool addition once he finally showed up, and those scenes helped pick things up again.

Overall, Brave New World is worth watching, especially if you are a Marvel fan. The character work is strong and the movie delivers in terms of visuals and energy. But Marvel really needs to decide whether it wants to keep things grounded for its human heroes or start giving them a little more to work with. It is a good film, just not a perfect one.
